The meaning that fits the sentence, first

A Chrome extension that defines the word you double-click on any web page, from a copy of the English Wiktionary kept on your computer, and lists the meaning that fits what you are reading first.

Download for ChromeSource on GitHub

Free and open source. Not in the Chrome Web Store yet: see how to install.

Why another dictionary

Most dictionary extensions list meanings in the dictionary’s own order, so you read through five of them to find the one you need. Contextual Dictionary reads the sentence around the word and puts the best fit at the top. No meaning is hidden.

  • Ordered by context

    A small word-sense model reads up to 120 characters on each side of the word and sorts each entry’s meanings by how well they fit.

  • Private

    The dictionary and the model run on your computer. What you select is never sent anywhere. No account, no analytics.

  • Offline

    About 1.2 million English headwords with examples, from Wiktionary. After two downloads that you confirm, it needs no network.

On any page

Double-click a word, or select up to four. A card opens beside it with up to three entries, three meanings per entry and one example per meaning. “Show all meanings” reveals the rest. Escape closes it. Switch on “Show order details” to see where the dictionary had each meaning.

Install

Chrome 124 or later, on a desktop computer.

  1. Download contextual-dictionary-<version>.zip from the latest release and unzip it into a folder you will keep.
  2. Open chrome://extensions, switch on Developer mode, click Load unpacked and choose the folder.
  3. Open the extension’s window from the toolbar and confirm the two downloads it offers: the dictionary (66.5 MB) and the context model (147 MB).

Good to know

  • English only, for definitions and for the text you read.
  • The ordering is a help, not an oracle. On 2,300 test sentences it put the right meaning first 55% of the time, against 28% for dictionary order (how it was measured).
  • While the model is in use the extension needs about 1 GB of memory. It is released after ten idle minutes.
  • It does not run on Chrome’s own pages, the Chrome Web Store, PDFs or inside text fields.
  • What it reads, keeps and sends is on the privacy page.
